The offices we clean downtown and along Okeechobee Boulevard

West Palm Beach has the densest office market in the county, and it is not one market. There are Class A towers on the waterfront with a building engineer and a written vendor policy. There are 1980s suites off Australian Avenue with carpet tile and tinted glass. There are converted storefronts near Northwood where the rules are whatever gets agreed on the phone. Each needs a different arrangement.

What matters more than square footage is who walks through the door. A five-person title company with no public traffic is a light job. A ground-floor suite that sees fifty visitors a day is not. We ask about headcount, visitor volume, restroom count and the hours the space is actually occupied before anyone quotes anything.

  • Single suites and whole floors in downtown towers
  • Law firms, title companies, accountants and financial offices
  • Public-facing and government-adjacent offices with waiting areas
  • Older office parks and converted storefront units
  • Small multi-tenant buildings with shared restrooms and lobbies
  • One-off cleans after a suite move, a build-out or a long vacancy

Law offices: paper, conference rooms and the desk we do not touch

There are a lot of lawyers in West Palm Beach, which is what happens when you put the county courthouse in the middle of town. Legal offices are an easy space to clean and an easy space to get wrong. The rule we work to is simple: we do not move, stack, straighten or read paper. Files stay where they were left. If a conference table is covered in exhibits, that table gets left alone and everything around it gets cleaned.

Confidentiality is the condition of the work, not a favour. Florida Bar guidance is clear that a lawyer's duty to supervise reaches nonlawyers both inside and outside the firm, and a cleaning vendor sits squarely outside it. So locked file rooms stay locked unless you say otherwise, shred bins are handled only as the written scope allows, and we keep the same crew on your suite rather than sending new faces every Tuesday.

Public-facing and government offices: the lobby is the job

If the public comes to you, the waiting room is your reputation before anyone speaks. Chairs get touched all day. The counter gets leaned on. The glass partition collects handprints at exactly average shoulder height, and the restroom takes traffic that no five-person office would ever generate. Those rooms need attention on a different frequency to the back office, and a sensible scope says so instead of treating the whole floor as one number.

Public buildings usually add process: sign-in, escorted access, a badge that only works certain hours, and a written schedule the facilities manager can hold you to. That suits us. We would rather work to a clear document than a vague understanding. The EPA's guide for office building occupants is a reasonable primer if you are drafting a specification and want to know which complaints are cleaning problems and which are ventilation ones.

Freight elevators, loading docks and a building that turns the air off at seven

In a downtown tower, the cleaning schedule is only half yours. The building controls the loading dock hours, the freight elevator, the compactor, the janitor closet and whether after-hours air conditioning costs the tenant extra. That last one is not a small detail. Cleaning a sealed twelfth floor in August with the HVAC on a night setback is warm, slow work, and it is better to plan for it than discover it.

So before we start we get the boring answers: dock access times, whether the freight elevator needs reserving, where waste and recycling actually go, which entrance is open after hours, whether building management needs our insurance certificate on file, and who to call when a door does not open. Then the crew is not standing on Datura Street at nine at night calling somebody's cell phone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you clean our downtown office after hours, and what will the building need from us? Yes, evenings, overnight and early mornings all work. Most towers want the vendor listed with building management, an insurance certificate on file, and access arranged through the freight elevator and dock within set hours. Send us your building's vendor requirements and we will deal with the property manager directly.
Do you clean law offices, and will your crew go near client files? We clean legal offices, and our rule is that paper does not get moved, tidied or read. Desks with files on them are cleaned around, not cleared. Locked rooms stay locked unless your written scope says otherwise, and shred bins are only handled if you have asked for it in the scope.
How is office and workplace cleaning in West Palm Beach priced? From a walkthrough, then flat for the agreed scope. The main drivers are square footage, how many nights a week, restroom count, floor types and how much public traffic the space takes. No hourly meter that speeds up in August. If the scope changes later, we re-quote it rather than quietly absorbing or dropping work.
Can you work in a building that requires freight elevator reservations? Yes, and we would rather know on day one than on night one. Tell us the reservation process, the dock hours and who holds the key to the janitor closet. We schedule the crew inside those windows. Buildings that run tightly are easier to clean, not harder.
Do you clean offices and homes for the same customer? Often. Plenty of people book residential and commercial cleaning services in West Palm Beach from us on the same call: the office on a weekly night schedule, the house bi-weekly during the day. Two scopes, two schedules, one company, one number to ring when something needs changing.
Do you handle carpets, tile and windows in commercial spaces? We take carpet, tile, upholstery and window work, quoted after we see the job. Commercial carpet in a busy suite and a lobby tile floor are not the same task, and neither is priced sensibly over the phone. Tell us what you have and we will come and look.
How often does a small West Palm Beach office actually need cleaning? It depends on headcount and visitors, not on what a competitor sold you. A quiet suite with under ten staff is often fine on two or three nights a week, with restrooms the deciding factor. Public-facing space with steady footfall usually needs daily attention to restrooms and the lobby.
