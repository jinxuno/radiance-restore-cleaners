Professional Disinfection Service in Palm Beach, FL

Serving Palm Beach, Port St. Lucie, and Fort Pierce

Home›Cleaning Guides›Disinfection Service

High-touch surfaces disinfected throughout the home: handles, switches, remotes, railings, faucets, and counters. Available on its own or added to any cleaning visit.

This gets booked two ways. Some clients add it onto a regular cleaning because they want handles, switches and railings done properly rather than passed over with the same cloth as the coffee table. Others book it on its own, usually in the week before guests arrive or the week after a full house empties out. Standing alone it is a short, focused visit, which makes it easy to slot into the gap between recurring cleans. What it does not do is replace cleaning. A disinfectant needs a clean surface underneath it to work the way its label says, so the visit is really two passes across the same list of touch points, and that second pass is where the time goes.

What's Included

  • Door handles, levers, push plates and door frames wiped and disinfected room by room
  • Light switches, outlet plates, remotes and thermostat panels cleaned and then gone over again
  • Stair banisters and handrails cleaned and disinfected end to end, spindles and newel posts included
  • Faucets, sink basins, toilet handles and flush buttons disinfected, chrome shined afterward
  • Kitchen counters, backsplash, cabinet pulls and appliance handles cleaned first, then wiped down again
  • EPA-registered products applied per the label, including the full dwell time on every surface
  • Refrigerator and microwave handles, oven handles and the dishwasher control panel all covered as well
  • Slider handles, patio door latches and entry hardware included where you want them done

Clean First, Disinfect Second, Then Wait

Every touch point gets two passes. The first lifts the film that builds on a handle or a switch plate, which is skin oil, hand cream, cooking grease and dust bound together. A disinfectant applied straight on top of that film sits on the film instead of the surface. So the first cloth cleans, the second applies the product, and then the surface is left visibly wet for the contact time the label prints. That waiting period is the whole point, and it is not something you can wipe your way out of.

We move room by room in a fixed order rather than wandering the house, because touch points are small and easy to miss when you are jumping around. Handles get the underside and the shaft, not just the top. Railings are done end to end, including the section by the newel post that everyone grabs. Switch plates get the screws and edges. Cheaper visits tend to hit the obvious front faces and stop there, which leaves the parts hands actually wrap around.

Why This Matters in Palm Beach

Plenty of Palm Beach houses sit closed from May through October and then fill up inside a week. Somebody turns the air conditioning down, the shutters come off, family and guests arrive, and every handle, railing and light switch goes from untouched to constantly used in a matter of days. We book a lot of these visits in October and again in April, on either side of the season. A house that has been shut also has a summer of settled dust on those same surfaces, so the cleaning pass matters more here than it would in a home lived in year round. The same pattern shows up in the guest wings and pool houses that only open when family visits, and in the properties near Worth Avenue that swing between empty and full several times a season. We move through the house room by room so nothing gets skipped, clean each surface before disinfecting it, and let the product sit for the contact time its label calls for. It pairs naturally with the first full clean of the season.

Mistakes That Undo a Disinfection Visit

The big one is drying the product off early. If a counter is wiped down thirty seconds after the spray, the label conditions were never met and the step effectively did not happen. The second is disinfecting a surface that was never cleaned, which looks identical from across the room and is not. The third is treating it as a substitute for a proper clean. It is an add-on to cleaning, not a replacement for it.

On the household side, the usual miss is putting hands straight back on everything. Steering wheels, phones and keys travel with people, and the front door handle gets touched again on the way in. That is normal and not something a cleaning visit fixes. The sensible pattern is to schedule the visit close to when it matters, the day before guests arrive rather than a fortnight ahead, and add it to your recurring clean in between.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I book disinfection without a full cleaning? Yes. It works on its own or as an add-on to any cleaning visit. Standing alone it is a shorter appointment focused only on high-touch surfaces, which suits the gap between regular cleans or the day before guests land. It still includes the cleaning pass on those surfaces first.
What products do you use? EPA-registered disinfectants, used per the label directions including the contact time the label requires. If someone in the house is sensitive to fragrance, tell us when you book and we bring lower-fume options wherever we carry a suitable one. We will tell you what we used on which surfaces if you ask.
Do you disinfect electronics? We wipe remotes, switch plates and thermostat panels. Laptops, televisions, screens and anything with exposed ports we clean around rather than on, unless you leave the manufacturer instructions telling us what is safe to apply. Getting liquid into a port is a fast way to ruin something expensive.
Do we need to stay out of the rooms while you work? No, but give each surface a few minutes. Disinfectants have to stay wet for the label dwell time, so handles, switches and counters will be damp behind us. We work room by room, so following one room back is fine. If fumes are a concern, ask for lower-fume products when you book.
How long does it hold? Only until the surfaces are used again, which in a busy house means the same day. This is not a treatment that stays on a handle. It is a reset. That is why most clients time it around something specific, guests arriving or a house reopening, or attach it to a recurring clean rather than booking it randomly.
