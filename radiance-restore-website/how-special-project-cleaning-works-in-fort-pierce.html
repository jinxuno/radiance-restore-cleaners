How Special Project Cleaning Works in Fort Pierce, FL

Serving Palm Beach, Port St. Lucie, and Fort Pierce

Home›Cleaning Guides›Special Project Cleaning

One-off jobs that don't fit a standard scope: post-renovation, hoarding recovery, seasonal cleanouts, or a single room that needs serious attention. Quoted after we see it.

Special project work gets booked when a job does not fit a checklist. A rental that came back worse than expected. A spare room that turned into storage over four years. The week after a contractor finally packs up. A family home that has to be cleared before it goes on the market. Jobs like these vary too widely to price off square footage, so they run on a different track. We look at the space, agree in writing on what is included, and quote a flat number before anyone starts. That protects both sides. You know exactly what you are paying, and we know what we are walking into, which means the crew shows up with the right equipment instead of improvising.

What's Included

  • Walk-through first, with a written scope and a flat price agreed before any work starts
  • Post-renovation dust taken off walls, trim, window and door tracks, and light fixtures
  • Single rooms brought back: baseboards hand-washed, walls spot-treated, ceiling fans wiped down
  • Seasonal cleanouts of garages, spare rooms, and storage areas, sorted before anything is cleaned
  • Hoarding recovery handled in stages, at a pace you set, without judgment
  • Household trash bagged and taken to your bins; construction debris haul-away quoted separately
  • Blinds, shutters, and window and door tracks deep cleaned where the project calls for it
  • Appliance exteriors, cabinet fronts, and handles washed once the surrounding dust is down
  • Work split across several visits when one day is not enough to finish it properly

Why the Walk-Through Comes First

The walk-through takes about twenty minutes and settles everything. We open the room, look at the surfaces, check whether the dust has settled or is still moving around, and ask what the space needs to be ready for. Then you get a scope in writing: rooms, surfaces, what is included and what is not. Nothing about a special project is standard, so a phone estimate is a guess, and a guess turns into an awkward conversation about money halfway through the afternoon.

The work itself runs top down and dry before wet. Ceilings, fans, and light fixtures first, then walls and trim, then tracks and sills, then floors, because any other order means cleaning the same floor twice. Renovation dust gets vacuumed with a HEPA-filter machine before a cloth touches it, since wiping dry drywall dust just grinds it into the finish. Microfiber gets swapped constantly. That last part is most of the labor and most of what a cheap quote leaves out.

Why This Matters in Fort Pierce

Fort Pierce generates a run of special project work every hurricane season. Houses on Hutchinson Island and along the lagoon come through a storm with water tracked down the hallway, lanai furniture in pieces, and a fine grit across every surface from windows that were boarded or briefly open. Shutters that spent a week closed come down with their tracks packed solid. Garages get emptied into the house before a storm and stay that way for a month afterward, which is how a spare bedroom quietly becomes a storage room. Add the seasonal cleanouts that come when a family property in the Historic Downtown district changes hands, often after years of one person living in a house built for six, and most of these jobs look nothing like a normal visit. So we walk the house first, tell you plainly what the work involves, and quote a flat price before anyone starts. Construction debris haul-away stays a separate add-on, so you know upfront what is and is not part of the number.

Setting the Pace on a Multi-Visit Project

Big projects go better in stages. We agree on what gets done in each visit and what finished looks like at the end of it, which keeps a four-day job from feeling open-ended. Rooms get completed one at a time rather than pushed to half-done everywhere, because a finished room gives you somewhere to stand and a reason to keep going. You approve the plan and the price for each stage before that stage starts.

On cleanouts and recovery work, decisions set the pace, not cleaning. We move at whatever speed you can make calls at, and anything you are unsure about stays where it is instead of getting sorted out from under you. Paperwork, photographs, and anything that looks like it matters gets gathered and set aside for you. Nothing is discarded that you have not looked at. If a stage is running long, we say so before the day ends.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why can't you quote this over the phone? Because the scope varies too much. Two rooms that sound identical on the phone can be an afternoon apart in real work. We look at the space, tell you what we would do, and give you a flat price before booking. The walk-through costs you nothing and takes about twenty minutes of your day.
Do you remove furniture or construction debris? Household trash goes out with your regular bins. We are not a hauling company, so furniture removal is not part of this. Debris haul-away after construction work is a separate add-on we can quote alongside the cleaning. Bulk pickup of large items is usually handled through your municipal service.
Do you really take on hoarding recovery? Yes, and we treat it as ordinary work. It usually runs across several visits so nothing gets rushed and you have time to decide what stays. We agree the plan and the price for each stage before we start, and nothing is thrown out without you seeing it first.
Is this the same as post-construction cleaning? Not quite. Post-construction cleaning is a defined service for a finished build or remodel, with a full dust inspection and a set checklist. Special project work covers what does not fit a standard scope: partial renovations, single rooms, cleanouts. If your job is a whole finished build, the post-construction service is usually the better fit.
What do you need from me before the crew arrives? Power, running water, a place to park, and access to every room in the scope. Tell us about anything fragile, any surface that is already damaged, and any area that is off limits. If pets are home, a closed door works fine. Beyond that there is nothing you need to pre-clean or move.

Every special project starts the same way. Somebody looks at a room and does not know where to begin. That part is what we take off you. You get a walk-through, a written scope, a flat price, and work that moves at a pace you set. Call, or send a few photos with a note about the space, and we will arrange a time to come and see it.
