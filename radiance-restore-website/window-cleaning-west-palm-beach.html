Storefront window cleaning and the first impression on Clematis Street

A storefront's glass is public property in every sense but the legal one. Fingerprints at handle height, dog nose prints at knee height, and a film that catches the low afternoon sun in exactly the spot your window display sits. Businesses on Clematis Street, in Northwood Village and along the antique row on South Dixie deal with all of that plus foot traffic that never really stops.

Entrance doors take the heaviest use in most businesses and are usually the first thing to look tired by Thursday. The CDC's hygiene guidance covers routine cleaning of frequently touched surfaces, and a door handle and push plate are about as frequently touched as anything gets. Clean glass is partly about appearance and partly the same simple idea. Doors are also where most people first notice that the rest of the glass needs doing.

Why West Palm Beach glass does not stay clean

Buildings near the water pick up a fine salt film that a dry cloth mostly redistributes. Add the wet season, when afternoon storms dry into mineral spotting, then pollen in spring and construction dust from whatever is going up downtown this year. Glass here does not gently dull over a season. It goes off in a fortnight. Anyone who has cleaned their own shopfront on a Sunday already knows this.

West-facing glass suffers most, because the sun that shows every mark also bakes whatever is sitting on the surface. East-facing glass near the Intracoastal takes the morning salt instead. None of this is cause for despair. It is a reason to be realistic about frequency, rather than cleaning once in March and wondering why it did not hold. Build the interval around the building you actually have, not the one in the brochure.

How often should storefront and office windows be cleaned?

There is no universal answer, and anyone offering one has not seen your building. What matters is how close you are to the water, how many people come through the door, whether there is food service, and how much of the glass sits at eye level. A restaurant entrance and a third-floor office window do not age at anything like the same rate.

Most businesses find their interval within the first couple of months. Book one clean, look at the glass again three weeks later, and set the schedule from what you see rather than from a brochure. We would rather build the frequency around your building than sell you a plan you do not need and will quietly cancel in the autumn.

Storm panels, shutters and the glass afterwards

Atlantic hurricane season runs 1 June to 30 November, and the peak of it falls around 10 September, with most activity between mid-August and mid-October. In West Palm Beach that means panels and shutters going up and coming down, sometimes more than once in a season, and glass spending days behind metal collecting whatever the wind brought with it. Panels also scuff the frames on the way on and off, which is worth a look while you are there.

Afterwards, businesses usually want the glass done before anything else, because it is what the street sees first. Ring us and we will tell you honestly how soon we can get to you. If there is something on the glass that is not dirt, describe it. Some marks come off and some are in the glass for good, and we would rather say so before taking your money. Etched or pitted glass is a replacement job, not a cleaning one.

How we quote, and what we will not claim

You will not find equipment lists, chemical names or height capabilities on this page, and it is worth being a little wary of any company that publishes them without having seen your building. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does commercial window cleaning in West Palm Beach cost? It depends on the number and size of the panes, the height, how the glass is reached, whether the interior is included, and how often you want it done. We quote flat once we know those things, and the quote itself costs nothing. Call (561) 556-5899 or email admin@radiancerestore.org with the details.
Do you clean upper-floor or high-rise windows? Tell us the building and how the glass is reached and we will answer plainly rather than optimistically. Working at height is regulated for good reason, and we would rather decline a job than take one we should not. For a lot of storefront and low-rise office work here, the answer is straightforward.
How often should storefront windows be cleaned? More often than most owners expect, and it varies by location and door traffic. Instead of guessing, book one clean and look at the glass again about three weeks later. That tells you your real interval. Premises near the water and those with constant foot traffic almost always need it more often.
Can you clean before we open or after we close? Yes. We are open 24 hours a day, seven days a week, and most commercial glass is done outside trading hours anyway. Tell us the window you want us in, including early mornings, evenings or weekends, and we will schedule inside it rather than around it.
Do you clean the inside of the windows as well as the outside? That is part of the scoping conversation rather than an assumption. Interior glass changes the time needed and the access arrangements, so we confirm it before quoting. If you want both, say so when you get in touch and it will be in the price you are given, not added later.
Can you do the windows and clean the rest of the premises? Yes. We take commercial and janitorial work alongside window cleaning, quoted together or separately, whichever suits how you budget. Some clients start with the glass and add the rest once they have seen how we work. There is no requirement to bundle anything. Tell us what you need and we will price the pieces separately so you can see what each part costs.
Do you clean residential windows in West Palm Beach? Yes, on the same basis: describe the house and we will quote the job. It often makes sense to combine window cleaning with a deep clean or a move-out clean, so everything happens on one visit rather than three separate appointments in three separate weeks.
