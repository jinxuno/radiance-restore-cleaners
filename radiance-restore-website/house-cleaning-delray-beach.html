House cleaning Delray Beach FL: sand, lovebugs and the season

Beach sand is the quiet one. It travels in on shoes and towels, settles into the grout lines of a tiled entryway, then acts like fine sandpaper every time somebody walks over it. Screened patios collect the same sand plus whatever the last squall took off the neighbour's ficus. Homes a few blocks from A1A also get a salt film on east-facing glass that a dry cloth just smears around.

Then there is the calendar. Lovebugs turn up twice a year, roughly April to May and again August to September, and they do not confine themselves to your bumper. Screens, patio furniture and the front door all take a share. Hurricane season runs June through November with the busiest stretch from mid-August to mid-October, which is also when a closed-up house gets stuffy fastest.

A few small habits make a visible difference between visits. A decent mat at every exterior door catches most of the sand before it reaches the grout. Running the air conditioner rather than opening the house on a humid day keeps the bathrooms drier. And rinsing the patio and the front walk after a lovebug week saves everyone a lot of scrubbing later, because they are far easier to remove before the sun bakes them on.

Terrazzo, jalousies and 1950s bathrooms: older Delray housing stock

A lot of the older housing east of Swinton has terrazzo under the carpet or the laminate, original jalousie windows in the Florida room, and a bathroom tiled in a colour that has been back in fashion twice. These houses are lovely and they are also fussy. Terrazzo shows every streak. Jalousie slats have twice the edges of a normal window and collect grime on all of them.

Newer construction out west has its own habits: high ceilings that put fan blades out of reach, big porcelain tile that shows dust in raking afternoon light, and a laundry room where a sock has been living behind the dryer since the second Obama administration. We do not treat a 1956 ranch and a 2015 townhouse as the same job, because they are not.

Gate codes, HOAs and getting our cleaners to your door

Half of Delray lives behind a guardhouse. Communities west of Military Trail generally want visitors on an approved list, and a cleaner who is not on it will be turned around at the gate no matter how polite everyone is about it. Add us to your permanent guest list, or leave a call-ahead number with the gate, and the visit runs on time.

Some associations also have rules about vehicle parking, service hours and where a work vehicle can sit. Tell us your HOA's window when you book and we will schedule inside it. If you are up north for the summer and want the house checked and cleaned before you fly back, that works too. Plenty of Delray homes only see their owners between November and April.

  • Tell us your HOA's permitted service hours

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does house cleaning cost in Delray Beach? It depends on square footage and whether you want a standard or a deep clean. We quote a flat price upfront from your real measurements, and our homepage has an instant calculator so you can see a figure yourself. Recurring plans take 20% off weekly, 15% off bi-weekly and 10% off monthly.
Do I need to be home during the cleaning? No. Many Delray clients leave a key, a code or a lockbox and come home to a finished house. If you live behind a gate, put us on your community's approved visitor list first, otherwise the guardhouse will send our cleaner back down the road.
Should I start with a deep clean or a standard clean? If nobody has done the grout, baseboards and cabinet tops in the past year, start with a deep clean and then keep it steady with standard visits. It usually works out cheaper over twelve months than asking a routine visit to fix a year of build-up in three hours.
How often should a Delray Beach home be cleaned? Bi-weekly suits most households here and is what the majority of our Delray clients book. Homes with pets, sand-tracking kids or a lot of open tile often move to weekly. Snowbird properties that sit empty for months usually do best with a deep clean before arrival and standard visits through the season, then a final clean before the house is closed up again.
Are your cleaners background-checked and insured? Yes. Every Radiance Restore cleaner is background-checked, reference-checked and interviewed in person before they set foot in a client's home. They are insured and trained. We are a family-run company rather than a franchise, so the hiring standard is simply the one we would want applied to our own house, which turns out to be a useful test.
Can you clean a seasonal home that has been closed up all summer? Yes, and it is one of our busier requests every October. A house shut up through a Florida summer needs more than a wipe-down, so most owners book a deep clean timed to land a day or two before they fly in, then switch to standard visits once they are in residence. Give us a couple of weeks of notice in season, because those slots go quickly.
What if something gets missed on the visit? Let us know within 24 hours and we come back and re-clean the area free. No debate, no invoice, no form to fill in.
