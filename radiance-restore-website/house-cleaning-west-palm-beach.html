What our cleaning services in West Palm Beach include

A standard visit covers the rooms you live in: kitchen surfaces and appliance exteriors, bathrooms, floors, dusting, glass, mirrors and bins. It is a maintenance clean, and it does its best work on a schedule. A deep clean goes after what builds up quietly behind that — grout, baseboards, fan blades, vent covers, the tops of the kitchen cabinets, which nobody sees and everybody forgets. Plenty of West Palm Beach households start with one deep clean and then settle into a routine.

We also clean empty properties before a move, turn over short-term rentals between guests, and handle post-construction cleans after a remodel. That last one is its own animal. Renovation dust finds every drawer track and then comes back a day later to prove a point. Carpet, tile, upholstery and window work we now take as well, and for those we quote after somebody has actually seen the job.

  • Kitchen: counters, sinks, cooktop, appliance fronts, cabinet faces
  • Bathrooms: showers, tubs, toilets, tile, mirrors, chrome
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped, tile and wood treated differently
  • Dusting: sills, ledges, blinds and fans within safe reach
  • Beds made, linens changed if you ask and leave them out
  • Bins emptied and relined
  • On request or as part of a deep clean: inside the oven, inside the fridge, grout, baseboards, vents

One city, two kinds of house: 1920s bungalows and downtown towers

West Palm Beach housing stock spans a century, and that is unusual for South Florida. Northwood, Flamingo Park, El Cid and Grandview Heights are full of homes from the 1920s and 1930s: plaster walls, narrow-board wood floors, hexagonal tile in the bathroom, casement windows that stopped being square around the Truman administration. They are beautiful and they are particular. Old finishes do not forgive a soaking mop or a cheap abrasive.

Downtown is a different job on the same map. Sealed glass, no windows that open, porcelain or engineered floors, and air conditioning that runs whether anyone is home or not. The dust is finer, the balcony slider track collects a grey line of grit, and everything arrives and leaves through a service elevator. Tell us which house you have when you book, because the products and the order of work change. On historic surfaces we would rather read the National Park Service preservation briefs than experiment on your floor.

Cleaning around a household that works nights

West Palm Beach keeps strange hours. Hospitals, the courthouse, county offices, hotels, and the kitchens along Clematis and Rosemary all run shifts, so in a lot of homes here somebody is asleep at two in the afternoon and awake at four in the morning. We are open 24 hours, seven days a week, and we build the schedule around the household rather than pretending everyone is free between nine and three.

That means the practical stuff gets agreed before the first visit. Which door, which code, whether the dog is friendly or merely tolerant, which bedroom stays shut, whether we start at the back of the house and work forward. If a visit misses the mark, say so within 24 hours and we return and re-clean the room at no charge. We would rather hear it the same week than read it politely worded six months later.

Four o'clock rain and what a wet summer does indoors

From late May into October the rain turns up most afternoons without knocking. The National Weather Service office in Miami tracks South Florida's wet season, and it is dependable enough to plan around. Indoors it shows as water tracked in at the door, a mat that never fully dries, grit on tile that turns into a film, and a laundry room that smells like a towel forgotten in a hot car.

Humidity is the real culprit, not dirt. A musty smell is moisture doing something, and the EPA is blunt about it: control the moisture and you control the mould. We will clean the shower ceiling, the window tracks and the closet shelves, and we will tell you honestly when what you actually need is a dehumidifier or an air conditioner that runs longer. Selling you a fourth clean for a plumbing problem would be a poor way to keep a customer.

Renting in West Palm Beach: duplexes, garage apartments and turnovers

A large share of this city is rented. Old houses carved into duplexes, garage apartments behind the main house, downtown units on twelve-month leases, and a reliable rush of people moving in June and again in September. Landlords and property managers book us directly for the gap between tenants, when the place is empty and everything is reachable. Empty is faster, and faster is cheaper, which is one of the few things in Florida that works in your favour.

Short-term rentals are their own rhythm. A turnover is not a house clean with a different name: there is a checkout time, an arrival time, linens to reset and a quiet expectation that you will report the cracked lamp rather than arrange it artfully. We do those too, and we do them on the clock.

We just bought a 1920s house downtown. Should the first visit be a deep clean? Usually, yes. Older homes hold decades of build-up in grout lines, window channels, vents and the tops of cabinets, and a standard visit is not designed to reach all of it.
