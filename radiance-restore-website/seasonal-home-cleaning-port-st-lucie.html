What five months closed actually does to a Port St. Lucie house

The smell hits first. It is not usually mould, and it is not usually anything dramatic. It is stillness. The same air pushed around by the same air handler since spring, soaked into carpet, sofa cushions and the inside of every closed cabinet. Newer Port St. Lucie construction is built tight, which is good for your power bill and unhelpful for airing out. Open the sliders, get the ceiling fans moving, and the house starts to smell like a house again within an hour.

Then you start seeing things. A fine grey film on tile that only shows up when the low October sun comes through the slider. Cobwebs in the corners of the lanai. Lovebug residue baked onto the screens from the September flight, because Florida schedules those twice a year and nobody asked us. Gecko droppings on a windowsill. And a guest bathroom that smells faintly of sewer, because the water in a trap nobody used since May simply evaporated.

  • Grey dust film on tile, counters and the tops of doors
  • Cobwebs in lanai corners and around outdoor light fixtures
  • Lovebug and pollen residue on window screens
  • Evaporation rings in toilet bowls and unused sinks
  • Dry drain traps in the guest bath and laundry
  • Ant trails to whatever the pantry still had in it
  • Patio furniture with a season of grit on it

The order we work an arrival cleaning in Port St. Lucie

Most cleaning goes top to bottom, dry before wet. An open-up clean breaks that rule on purpose. The first thing we do is run water briefly in every sink, tub, shower and floor drain, and flush every toilet. That refills the traps, and the sewer smell in the guest bath is gone hours before you land. Then the sliders open, the fans go on, and the house gets some moving air while we work. It sounds small. It is the difference between walking into your house and walking into a closed one.

After that it is ordinary, careful work in a sensible order. Ceiling fan blades and high shelves first, then blinds, sills and light fixtures, then every flat surface, then the kitchen and bathrooms, then floors last so nothing lands on clean tile. The refrigerator gets wiped inside and out and the door gets shut. Beds get made up with whatever linens you left, or the ones you shipped ahead. Lanai and pool deck are on the list if you want them, and on the Treasure Coast most people do.

  • Run every fixture and flush every toilet to refill dry traps
  • Open up and get air moving before anything else
  • Ceiling fans, high shelves, blinds, sills, fixtures
  • Surfaces, cabinet fronts, baseboards
  • Kitchen and bathrooms, including inside the refrigerator
  • Floors last, then beds made and towels out

Opening a Florida home for the season: what is yours and what is ours

Worth being plain about this. We are a cleaning company. We are not a home-watch service, not a property manager, not an HVAC contractor and not a mould remediation firm. Nobody here is checking on your house every Tuesday while you are in Michigan, and nobody is touching your thermostat or your water main. What we do is clean the house before you get here, and clean it again before you leave. The rest of what follows is homeowner advice, from people who know Florida buildings better than we do.

UF/IFAS Extension is the source worth reading. Their guidance for a seasonal Florida home is to leave the air conditioning set no higher than 85 degrees so it keeps cycling and pulling moisture out, and to keep indoor relative humidity under about 55 to 60 percent, which is where mould and bacteria get interested. The EPA adds a useful rule for what you find inside: a mouldy patch bigger than roughly three feet by three feet is a job for a remediation specialist, not a mop. We clean surface dust and grime and tell you honestly what we saw. We do not paint over something that needs a professional.

Booking your open-up clean around the flight

Arrivals on the Treasure Coast start trickling in early October and build hard through mid-November, and everyone wants the same three days before Thanksgiving. Book early if your dates are fixed. The useful trick is to schedule the clean for the day before you land rather than the morning of, so the house has a night to air out and you are not stepping over a vacuum with luggage in your hands. Our phones are answered around the clock, seven days a week, which matters more than it sounds when a flight moves at eleven at night.

October is also still hurricane season, which runs through November 30 with the busiest stretch from mid-August through mid-October, according to the National Hurricane Center. Plans move. If a storm pushes your arrival back a week, tell us and we will move the date without drama. And if you want someone through the house once in the middle of the season, whether that is January guests or a March visit from the grandchildren, just ask. It is a normal booking, not a special program.

What a second home cleaning in Port St. Lucie costs

Flat price, quoted upfront, based on the real square footage of your house. Frequently asked questions

How far ahead should I book an open-up clean in Port St. Lucie?

Two to four weeks is comfortable for October. For the mid-November stretch and the week before Thanksgiving, give us a month if you can. Those are the busiest dates of the season. If you are calling on short notice, still call. We answer 24 hours a day and we can often work something out.

Do I need to be there to let you in?

No. Most seasonal clients leave a lockbox code, a smart lock code, or a key with a neighbour. If your community has a gate or a guardhouse, add us to your visitor list for the date and send the gate details ahead of time. Tradition and PGA Village clients usually handle it that way.

Is an arrival clean a standard clean or a deep clean?

Usually a deep clean the first time. Five months of settled dust, dry traps and untouched bathrooms is more than a standard visit is built for. After that, if you book recurring visits through the season, standard cleans keep it where the deep clean left it. We will tell you which one your house actually needs.

Will you turn on the water or the air conditioning before I arrive?

No. We are cleaners, not a home-watch or property management service, and we do not touch water mains, thermostats or breaker panels. If the water is off at the meter when we arrive, we cannot clean, so have it turned on before our visit or arrange it with whoever handles the house for you.

My house smells musty. Is that mould?

Often it is just still air, and it clears once the house is open and moving. Sometimes it is not. The EPA notes that a persistent mouldy smell can mean hidden growth, and that anything larger than about a three by three foot patch should go to a remediation specialist. We clean. If we see something that needs one of those, we say so.

Do you clean the lanai, pool deck and screens?

Yes, as part of the quote if you ask for it. Cobwebs, patio furniture, the screen enclosure, the pavers and the sliders on both sides. On the Treasure Coast the outdoor room is half the house, and walking into a lanai you can sit down in on the first night is worth the extra thirty minutes.
